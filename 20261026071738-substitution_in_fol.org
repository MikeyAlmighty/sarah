#+title: Substitution in FOL

* Substitution in FOL

* Overview

*Substitution* is like "search and replace" in a word processor, but in ~FOL~ it is applied only to *free variables* in a formula \(X\).

** Substitution rules

Let \(X\) be any formula and \(*\) a term (a *variable* or a *constant*).

The operation \((x \leftarrow *)\) is read "replace \(x\) by \(*\)" or "\(*\) is substituted for \(x\)":

- If \(x\) does *not* occur free in \(X\), then \((x \leftarrow *)(X) = X\).
- If \(x\) *does* occur free in \(X\), then \((x \leftarrow *)(X)\) is obtained from \(X\) by replacing all *free* occurrences of \(x\) in \(X\) by \(*\).

*** Notation

If \(x\) occurs free in \(X\) we write \(X[x]\), so it is natural to write

\[
(x \leftarrow *)(X[x]) = X[*]
\]

for the result of replacing all free occurrences of \(x\) in \(X\) by \(*\).

** Examples

1. \(X = P(x) \wedge (\exists x)Q(x)\)

   \((x \leftarrow a)(P(x) \wedge (\exists x)Q(x)) = P(a) \wedge (\exists x)Q(x)\)

   Only the first \(x\) is free; the \(x\) in \((\exists x)Q(x)\) is bound, so it is unchanged.

2. \(X = P(x) \wedge (\forall y)Q(x, y)\)

   \((x \leftarrow z)(P(x) \wedge (\forall y)Q(x, y)) = P(z) \wedge (\forall y)Q(z, y)\)

   Both occurrences of \(x\) are free, so both are replaced.

** Summary

| Case                       | Result of \((x \leftarrow *)(X)\)                 |
|----------------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| \(x\) not free in \(X\)    | \(X\) (unchanged)                                 |
| \(x\) free in \(X\)        | all *free* occurrences of \(x\) replaced by \(*\) |
| Bound occurrences of \(x\) | never replaced                                    |
