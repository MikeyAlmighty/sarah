#+title: Conjugate Pairs

* Conjugate Pairs

*Complex conjugates* are pairs of complex numbers of the form:
a + bi  and  a - bi

They are identical except for the /sign/ of the imaginary part.

When you *multiply* or *add* a ~conjugate pair~, the imaginary parts cancel out and you're left with a real number:

- (a + bi) + (a - bi) = 2a (real)
- (a + bi)(a - bi) = a² + b² (real)

The conjugate of z is conventionally written z̄ (z-bar).

The reason they show up naturally in quadratics is the ± in the quadratic formula.

If the discriminant b² - 4ac is negative, you get √(negative) = some imaginary number, and the ± splits it into a conjugate pair.

So as long as the coefficients a, b, c are real, complex roots are guaranteed to come in conjugate pairs.
