#+title: Tower of Hanoi Induction

* Tower of Hanoi Induction

** Problem
Let $T(n)$ be the minimum number of moves to solve Tower of Hanoi with $n$ discs.

- $T(1) = 1$
- $T(2) = 3$
- Recurrence: move $n-1$ discs to auxiliary peg ($T(n-1)$), move largest disc ($1$), move $n-1$ discs from auxiliary to destination ($T(n-1)$)

$$T(n) = 2T(n-1) + 1$$

Closed form: $T(n) = 2^n - 1$

** Proof of Correctness (by Induction)

*** Base case
$n = 1$: one disc, move directly source → destination. Satisfies the rules. ✓

*** Inductive hypothesis
Assume the recursive solution correctly moves $k$ discs from source to destination for $n = k$.

*** Inductive step
Show it holds for $n = k + 1$:

1. Move top $k$ discs source → auxiliary (valid by IH)
2. Move disc $k+1$ source → destination (valid: it's the largest, nothing on top of it, destination clear of smaller discs)
3. Move $k$ discs auxiliary → destination (valid by IH)

Each sub-step follows the rules, so the full move sequence for $k+1$ discs is valid.

*** Conclusion
By induction, the recursive solution is correct for all $n \geq 1$.

** Why this matters

This is a standard example of using induction not just to prove a *formula* (like $2^n - 1$) but to verify the *correctness of an algorithm* - i.e., that a recursive procedure actually produces a valid solution satisfying all constraints, for every input size.
