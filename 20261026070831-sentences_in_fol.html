Sentences in FOL

Sentences in FOL

Overview

Occurrence of a variable

A variable may occur several times in a formula

  • Each occurrence is counted separately.

Example: \((\forall x)(Q(x, y) \rightarrow P(x)) \wedge \neg((\exists x)(P(x)) \wedge T(x, y))\)

  • \(x\) occurs six times (including the \(x\) in \((\forall x)\) and in \((\exists x)\)).
  • \(y\) occurs twice.

Each occurrence of a variable is in a different environment.

Scope of a quantifier

An occurrence of \((\forall x)\) or \((\exists x)\) determines a subformula, its scope:

  • The largest subformula over which the quantifier applies.

In the example above:

  • \((Q(x, y) \rightarrow P(x))\) is the scope of \((\forall x)\)
  • \(P(x)\) is the scope of \((\exists x)\)

Note: check the bracketing carefully. \(T(x, y)\) is not bracketed with \(P(x)\), so it is not in the scope of \((\exists x)\).

Bound and free variables

For each occurrence of a variable \(x\):

  • Bound: the occurrence is in a quantifier \((\forall x)\) / \((\exists x)\) (In scope).
  • Free: any other occurrence.

Important: a variable may have both free and bound occurrences in the same formula.

Example

Formula: \((P(x) \rightarrow Q(y)) \rightarrow (\exists y)R(x, y)\)

Occurrence (left to right) Status Reason
1st \(x\) (in \(P(x)\)) free not in scope of \(\forall x\) or \(\exists x\)
1st \(y\) (in \(Q(y)\)) free not in scope of \(\forall y\) or \(\exists y\)
2nd \(y\) (in \(\exists y\)) bound inside the quantifier
2nd \(x\) (in \(R(x, y)\)) free not in scope of \(\forall x\) or \(\exists x\)
last \(y\) (in \(R(x, y)\)) bound \(R(x, y)\) is in the scope of \((\exists y)\)
  • \(x\) occurs free twice
  • \(y\) occurs free once and bound twice.

Open and closed formulae

  • Open: has a free occurrence of some variable.
  • Closed: otherwise.

Definition 6.2: A closed formula is called a sentence.

Note: a sentence only requires no free variables.

  • There is no restriction on constants.

Example:

\((\exists x)(F(x) \wedge G(x)) \rightarrow ((\exists x)F(x) \wedge (\exists x)G(x))\) is a sentence, since every occurrence of \(x\) is bound.

In terms of parse trees

  • A formula is a sentence if there is a path from each variable \(x\) (as a leaf) up to a vertex labelled \(\forall x\) or \(\exists x\).

Notation

If \(X\) has free variables \(x_1, \dots, x_n\) with \(n \geq 1\), write \(X[x_1, \dots, x_n]\) (square brackets) to highlight that the formula has free variables.

Summary

Term Meaning
Occurrence Each appearance of a variable, counted separately
Scope Largest subformula a quantifier applies to
Bound Occurrence in a quantifier or its scope
Free Occurrence that is not bound
Open formula Has at least one free occurrence of a variable
Closed formula No free occurrences (= sentence)
\(X[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) Notation for \(X\) with free variables \(x_1,\dots,x_n\)
