1. The problem

Let \(\{a_n\}\) be the sequence defined by

\[a_1 = 1, \qquad a_{n+1} = a_n + 8n, \qquad n \geq 1\]

Prove by induction that \(a_n = (2n-1)^2\) for all positive integers \(n\).

2. Why this example is different

Earlier examples (sums of odd numbers, sums of integers, sums of powers of 2) built the link between \(P(k)\) and \(P(k+1)\) by bracketing a sum — splitting off its last term.

Here there is no sum to bracket. Instead, the recurrence itself is the bridge between \(a_k\) and \(a_{k+1}\) — it is given directly in the problem and plays the same structural role bracketing played before: it’s the one piece of information that connects consecutive terms, without which there’d be no legal way to get from the assumption to the target.

There are two separate formulas in play, doing different jobs:

  • The recurrence (\(a_{n+1}=a_n+8n\)) - how the sequence is actually built. True by definition, given, never needs proving.
  • The formula being proved (\(a_n=(2n-1)^2\)) - the claim, \(P(n)\). Not automatically true; that’s the whole point of the proof.

3. Statement \(P(n)\)

\[P(n): \quad a_n = (2n-1)^2\]

4. Base case: \(P(1)\)

\[a_1 = (2(1)-1)^2 = 1^2 = 1\]

Since \(a_1=1\) is given directly, this matches. \(P(1)\) holds.

5. Inductive hypothesis: assume \(P(k)\)

\[a_k = (2k-1)^2\]

6. Inductive step: show \(P(k+1)\) follows

6.1. Part 1 — write the target (substitute \(n=k+1\) into the formula)

Substitute into \(a_n=(2n-1)^2\): replace both occurrences of \(n\) (the subscript, and the one inside the bracket) with \((k+1)\):

\[a_{k+1} = \big(2(k+1)-1\big)^2\]

Simplify inside the bracket: \(2(k+1)-1 = 2k+2-1 = 2k+1\).

\[\text{Target: } \quad a_{k+1} = (2k+1)^2\]

This step uses only the formula — nothing to do with the recurrence yet.

6.2. Part 2 — get an equation for \(a_{k+1}\) from the recurrence

Recurrence: \(a_{n+1}=a_n+8n\), true for every \(n\geq 1\). To get an equation about \(a_{k+1}\) specifically, set \(n=k\) (since we need \(n+1=k+1\)):

\[a_{k+1} = a_k + 8k\]

This is a fact from the recurrence alone — no assumption used yet.

6.3. Part 3 — substitute the assumption \(P(k)\)

\(P(k)\) says \(a_k=(2k-1)^2\). Substitute:

\[a_{k+1} = (2k-1)^2 + 8k\]

6.4. Part 4 — algebra

Expand \((2k-1)^2 = 4k^2-4k+1\):

\[a_{k+1} = 4k^2-4k+1+8k = 4k^2+4k+1\]

Factor:

\[4k^2+4k+1 = (2k+1)^2\]

This matches the target from Part 1 exactly. \(P(k+1)\) follows from \(P(k)\).

7. Conclusion

By the principle of mathematical induction, \(a_n=(2n-1)^2\) for all positive integers \(n\).

8. The general pattern for recurrence + induction problems

Step What happens
1 Write \(P(k+1)\)’s claim by substituting \(n=k+1\) into the formula — this is the target, set aside untouched
2 Use the recurrence, substituting \(n=k\), to get \(a_{k+1}\) in terms of \(a_k\) — this is the bridge
3 Substitute the assumption \(P(k)\) for \(a_k\)
4 Do algebra until the result matches the target exactly
