Important Logical Equivalences

Important Logical Equivalences

Idea

Some logical equivalences are useful for reducing a wff to a simpler but logically equivalent wff.

All of them can be proved using truth tables.

Theorem 4.1

Law Equivalence
Double negation \(\neg\neg p \equiv p\)
Idempotence \(p \land p \equiv p\) and \(p \lor p \equiv p\)
Associativity \((p \land q) \land r \equiv p \land (q \land r)\) and \((p \lor q) \lor r \equiv p \lor (q \lor r)\)
Commutativity \(p \land q \equiv q \land p\) and \(p \lor q \equiv q \lor p\)
Distributivity \(p \land (q \lor r) \equiv (p \land q) \lor (p \land r)\) and \(p \lor (q \land r) \equiv (p \lor q) \land (p \lor r)\)
De Morgan's laws \(\neg(p \land q) \equiv \neg p \lor \neg q\) and \(\neg(p \lor q) \equiv \neg p \land \neg q\)
Absorption \(p \lor (p \land q) \equiv p\) and \(p \land (p \lor q) \equiv p\)

Important consequences of Theorem 4.1

  • Idempotence: \(p \land p \equiv p\) and \(p \lor p \equiv p\) mean we can "simplify" expressions that have "repetitions" of atoms.
  • Associativity: \((p \land q) \land r \equiv p \land (q \land r)\) means we can write simply \(p \land q \land r\) without ambiguity, since both bracketings give the same truth table. Similarly, we can write \(p \lor q \lor r\) without worrying about omitting brackets.
  • Commutativity: the order in which we carry out a sequence of conjunctions does not matter. Similarly for a sequence of disjunctions.
  • Exclusive or: commutativity and associativity also hold for \(\oplus\): \[ p \oplus q \equiv q \oplus p \qquad\text{and}\qquad p \oplus (q \oplus r) \equiv (p \oplus q) \oplus r. \]

Example

Using commutativity, associativity, and idempotence we can show

Claim

\[ p \land q \land r \land p \land r \land q \equiv p \land q \land r \]

Laws used

Commutativity
\( a \land b \equiv b \land a \)
Associativity
\( (a \land b) \land c \equiv a \land (b \land c) \)
Idempotence
\( a \land a \equiv a \)

Derivation

  1. Regroup (commutativity + associativity), so duplicates sit together: \[ p \land q \land r \land p \land r \land q \equiv (p \land p) \land (q \land q) \land (r \land r) \]
  2. Collapse each pair (idempotence): \[ (p \land p) \land (q \land q) \land (r \land r) \equiv p \land q \land r \]

Intuition

A conjunction is true only if every conjunct is true.

Repeating a conjunct adds no new requirement, so duplicates are redundant.

Notes

  • Associativity is why parentheses can be dropped in a chain of \( \land \).
  • The dual holds for \( \lor \): \( a \lor a \equiv a \).

Warning: these laws do not carry over to other connectives

Commutativity and associativity hold for \( \lor \), \( \land \), \( \oplus \), but not for \( \to \).

Example 2: \( \to \) is not commutative

\[ p \to q \not\equiv q \to p \]

Counterexample: \( p = T,\ q = F \)

p q p → q q → p
T F F T

Same inputs, different truth values.

Example 3: \( \to \) is not associative

\[ (p \to q) \to r \not\equiv p \to (q \to r) \]

Counterexample: \( p = q = r = F \)

p q r (p → q) → r p → (q → r)
F F F F T

Same inputs, different truth values.

Takeaway

To refute an equivalence, a single row where the two sides differ is enough.
