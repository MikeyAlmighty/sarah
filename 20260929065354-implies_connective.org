#+title: Implies Connective

* Implies Connective

- aka *Implication*

** Definition

The connective *implies* (written $p \to q$ or $p \Rightarrow q$) is a *binary connective*.

It reads "$p$ *implies* $q$" or "if $p$ *then* $q$".

It is ~false~ only when $p$ is ~true~ and $q$ is ~false~.

Here $p$ is the /hypothesis/ (antecedent) and $q$ is the /conclusion/ (consequent).

** Motivation: implication as a promise

Think of $p \to q$ as a *promise or contract*: it is ~false~ if the promise is broken, and ~true~ otherwise.

Example: "Alice cleans up her room" implies "Alice gets ice cream".
- Cleans room, gets ice cream: promise kept (T).
- Cleans room, no ice cream: promise broken (F).
- Doesn't clean room: the promise is never invoked, so it cannot be broken (T), whether or not she gets ice cream.

** Truth table

| $p$ | $q$ | $p \to q$ |
|-----+-----+-----------|
| T   | T   | T         |
| T   | F   | F         |
| F   | T   | T         |
| F   | F   | T         |

** Notes

- *Not* commutative: $p \to q \not\equiv q \to p$. This differs from =and=, =or=, =xor= and =iff=.
- When $p$ is ~false~, $p \to q$ is ~true~ regardless of $q$ (sometimes called [[id:271567aa-3097-48d2-bd6a-581e2663575c][Vacuous Truth]]).
- The only falsifying row is T, F.
