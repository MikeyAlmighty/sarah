#+title: OR Connective

* (Inclusive) OR Connective
- aka *Disjunction*.

** Definition

The connective *or* (written $p \lor q$) is a *binary connective* corresponding to the /inclusive/ use of "or" in everyday speech.

The compound statement "$p$ or $q$" is ~true~ when at least one of $p$, $q$ is ~true~.

** Motivation: ambiguity of English "or"

"Alice likes cheese /or/ Bob likes cheese" can mean either:
- At least one of them likes cheese, possibly both (inclusive).
- Exactly one of them likes cheese, not both (exclusive).

In conversation we can clarify.

In an artificial language ([[PL]]) we can't allow a connective with two readings, so we define two connectives:
- *or*: inclusive
- *xor*: exclusive

** Truth table

| $p$ | $q$ | $p \lor q$ |
|-----+-----+------------|
| T   | T   | T          |
| T   | F   | T          |
| F   | T   | T          |
| F   | F   | F          |

$p \lor q$ is ~false~ iff /both/ $p$ and $q$ are ~false~.

** Notes

- Commutative: $p \lor q \equiv q \lor p$.
- Contrast with [[id:dfeac7cd-c703-4665-8ba5-f6e863306ce8][And]]: and needs *both* ~true~, =or= needs /at least one/.
- Differs from [[id:791af3eb-ea5c-4458-85c8-9e7c93edf32f][XOR]] only on the row T, T.
