#+title: Division With Remainders

* Division With Remainders

For any positive integers \(a\) and \(b\), we can always write:

\[ b = a \cdot q + r \]

For \(b = a \cdot q + r\):

| Dividend | Divisor | Quotient | Remainder |
|----------+---------+----------+-----------|
| b        | a       | q        | r         |

where \(0 \leq r < a\) is the *remainder*. If \(r = 0\) then \(a \mid b\).

*Edge case — when \(b < a\):* the quotient is \(0\) and \(b\) is its own
remainder, since \(b\) goes into \(a\) zero times. For example with
\(b = 3\) and \(a = 11\):

\[ 3 = 11 \cdot 0 + 3 \]

| Dividend | Divisor | Quotient | Remainder |
|----------+---------+----------+-----------|
| 3        | 11      | 0        | 3         |

This is still consistent with \(0 \leq r < a\), since \(r = 3 < 11\).

For example, with \(b = 14\) and \(a = 3\):

\[ 14 = 3 \cdot 4 + 2 \]

| Dividend | Divisor | Quotient | Remainder |
|----------+---------+----------+-----------|
| 14       | 3       | 4        | 2         |
