#+title: Relations

* Relations

** Overview

*Relations* describe associations between elements of one or more *sets*.

They are used to *organise data* and *model structures* (e.g. database tables, network graphs).

Standard properties covered in this lesson:
- Reflexivity
- Symmetry
- Transitivity
- A graphical representation of relations.

** Definition

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be sets.

- A *relation from \(A\) to \(B\)* is a /subset/ of the Cartesian product:
  \[ R \subseteq A \times B \]
  - [[id:d27d88b0-d87a-477a-81c0-a772200c4fca][see Definition 7.4, Cartesian product]]

- A *relation on \(A\)* is a relation from \(A\) to \(A\), i.e. \(R \subseteq A \times A\).
- Notation: \((a,b) \in R\) is often written \(a \mathrel{R} b\).

** Examples

*** 1. Students and courses

\(A\) = students at Heriot-Watt

\(B\) = courses

\[ R = \{(a,b) \mid a \in A,\ b \in B,\ a \text{ is enrolled on } b\} \]

*** 2. Parenthood

Relation on the set of people:
\[ R = \{(a,b) \mid a \text{ is a parent of } b\} \]

*** 3. Finite relation from \(A\) to \(B\)

\(A = \{1,2,3,4\}\)

\(B = \{x,y\}\)

\[ R = \{(1,y),(2,x),(3,x),(4,y)\} \subseteq A \times B \]

*** 4. Relations on \(\mathbb{Z}\)

\[ R_1 = \{(a,b) \mid a \geq b\} \]
\[ R_2 = \{(a,b) \mid a - b = 4\} \]

*** 5. Divisibility on a finite set

\(A = \{1,2,3,4\}\):
\[ R = \{(a,b) \mid a \text{ divides } b\} \]
\[ R = \{(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,2),(3,3),(4,4),(2,4)\} \]
