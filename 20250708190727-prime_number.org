#+title: Prime Numbers

* Prime Numbers

An integer ~p > 1~ for which ~1~ and ~p~ are its only (positive) *factors*.

- The /smallest/ *prime* is ~2~.
- The number ~1~ has only *one* factor
- A prime number must have precisely *two factors*. (This is why 1 is not a prime number)

*Primes* are the building blocks of [[id:1001ff7b-e2dd-454f-b458-424d8d587d95][Natural Numbers ($\mathbb{N}$)]];

Every /positive/ *integer* can be uniquely expressed as a *product of primes* (*prime factorisation*).

** Examples

- \(12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 = 2^2 \times 3\)
- \(18 = 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 2 \times 3^2\)
- \(30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5\)
- \(45 = 3 \times 3 \times 5 = 3^2 \times 5\)
- \(60 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5\)
- \(84 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 7 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7\)
- \(100 = 2 \times 2 \times 5 \times 5 = 2^2 \times 5^2\)
- \(210 = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7\)

*** Special Cases

- A prime number is already its own prime factorisation:

  - \(13 = 13\)
  - \(29 = 29\)

- \(1\) has no prime factorisation
  (it is neither prime nor composite).
