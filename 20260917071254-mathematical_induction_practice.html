Recurrence Relations

Method reminder

For each exercise:

  1. State \(P(n)\).
  2. Base case — check \(P(1)\).
  3. Inductive hypothesis — write \(P(k)\).
  4. Inductive step:
    • (a) Target — substitute \(n=k+1\) into the formula, simplify, set aside.
    • (b) Bridge — substitute \(n=k\) into the recurrence.
    • (c) Substitute the assumption into the bridge.
    • (d) Algebra — simplify until it matches the target.
  5. Conclusion.

Question 1

Let \(\{a_n\}\) be the sequence defined by \(a_1 = 2\) and

\[a_{n+1} = a_n + 4n+2, \qquad n \geq 1\]

Prove by induction that \(a_n = 2n^2\) for all positive integers \(n\).

Question 2

Let \(\{a_n\}\) be the sequence defined by \(a_1 = 1\) and

\[a_{n+1} = a_n + 3n^2+3n+1, \qquad n \geq 1\]

Prove by induction that \(a_n = n^3\) for all positive integers \(n\).

Question 3

Let \(\{a_n\}\) be the sequence defined by \(a_1 = 6\) and

\[a_{n+1} = a_n + 4n+6, \qquad n \geq 1\]

Prove by induction that \(a_n = 2n^2+4n\) for all positive integers \(n\).

Question 4

Let \(\{a_n\}\) be the sequence defined by \(a_1 = 2\) and

\[a_{n+1} = 2a_n, \qquad n \geq 1\]

Prove by induction that \(a_n = 2^n\) for all positive integers \(n\).

Question 5

Let \(\{a_n\}\) be the sequence defined by \(a_1 = 0\) and

\[a_{n+1} = a_n + 2n, \qquad n \geq 1\]

Prove by induction that \(a_n = n^2-n\) for all positive integers \(n\).

Summation

Method reminder

For each exercise:

  1. State \(P(n)\).
  2. Base case — check \(P(1)\).
  3. Inductive hypothesis — write \(P(k)\).
  4. Inductive step:
    • (a) Target — substitute \(n=k+1\) into the formula, simplify, set aside.
    • (b) Bracket the LHS sum, isolating everything up to the k-th term (pure regrouping, no calculation yet).
    • (c) Use \(P(k)\) to replace the bracketed part with its RHS.
    • (d) Algebra — simplify until it matches the target.
  5. Conclusion.

Question 1

Prove by induction that for all positive integers \(n\):

\[2+4+6+\dots+2n = n(n+1)\]

Question 2

Prove by induction that for all positive integers \(n\):

\[1^2+2^2+3^2+\dots+n^2 = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\]

Question 3

Prove by induction that for all positive integers \(n\):

\[1+4+7+\dots+(3n-2) = \frac{n(3n-1)}{2}\]

Question 4

Prove by induction that for all positive integers \(n\):

\[1^3+2^3+3^3+\dots+n^3 = \left[\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right]^2\]

Question 5

Prove by induction that for all positive integers \(n\):

\[\frac{1}{1\cdot2}+\frac{1}{2\cdot3}+\dots+\frac{1}{n(n+1)} = \frac{n}{n+1}\]

Note: the terms are fractions here rather than integers, but the same bracketing method applies — isolate the sum up to the k-th term, replace using \(P(k)\), then combine with the new term algebraically (common denominator).
