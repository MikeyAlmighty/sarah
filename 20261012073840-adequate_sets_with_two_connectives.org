#+title: Adequate Sets With Two Connectives

* Adequate Sets With Two Connectives

** Question

Is there an *adequate set* of *connectives* containing only /two/ connectives?
- See [[id:ef50162e-9514-4a64-9119-b933b9d4c588][Adequate Connectives]] for the definition of *adequate*.

** Key equivalences

Both follow from *De Morgan's* laws and *double negation*:
1. \(p \lor q \equiv \neg(\neg p \land \neg q)\)
2. \(p \land q \equiv \neg(\neg p \lor \neg q)\)

** Eliminating one connective

- *Omit \(\lor\):* by (1), \(A \lor B \equiv \neg(\neg A \land \neg B)\), so everything can be expressed with \(\neg\) and \(\land\).
- *Omit \(\land\):* by (2), \(A \land B \equiv \neg(\neg A \lor \neg B)\), so everything can be expressed with \(\neg\) and \(\lor\).

Combined with Proposition 4.2 (every wff is equivalent to one using only \(\neg, \lor, \land\)), this gives Proposition 4.3.

** Proposition 4.3

1. The connectives \(\neg\) and \(\land\) together form an *adequate set*.
2. The connectives \(\neg\) and \(\lor\) together form an *adequate set*.
