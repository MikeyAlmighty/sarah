#+title: Complex Numbers

* Complex Numbers

** Definition

A *complex number* is any number of the form:

#+begin_example
z = a + bi
#+end_example

where a, b ∈ ℝ and i = √(-1).

The set of *all* complex numbers is denoted *ℂ*.

- ~a~ is called the *real part* of z, written Re(z)
- ~b~ is called the *imaginary part* of z, written Im(z)

** Special Cases

| Condition | Type              |
|-----------+-------------------|
| b = 0     | Real number       |
| a = 0     | Imaginary number  |
| a,b ≠ 0   | Properly complex  |

In other words: ℝ ⊂ ℂ and imaginary numbers ⊂ ℂ. Both are subsets of the
complex numbers.

** Visualisation

Unlike ℝ, complex numbers cannot be ordered on a number line.

Instead they are visualised on the *complex plane* (Argand diagram), with:

- the horizontal axis representing the real part
- the vertical axis representing the imaginary part

** Computer Representation

A complex number z = a + bi is stored as a pair of real numbers ~(a, b)~, or in practice a pair of *floats*.
- They are approximations subject to floating-point precision limits.

** Algebraic Significance

- ℂ is an *algebraically closed field*:
  - every polynomial equation with coefficients in ℂ has a solution in ℂ.

This is the content of the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra.

The simplest motivating case is x² + 1 = 0, which has no solution in ℝ but has solutions ±i in ℂ.

The step from ℝ → ℂ is formally straightforward but conceptually significant
— it is the last extension of the number system most mathematicians need.
