#+title: Understanding Interpretations in FOL

* Understanding Interpretations in FOL

** Notation

Let \(I\) be a /correspondence/ between a language \(L\) and a structure \(\mathrm{D}\).

If \(X\) is a formula in \(L\), then
- \(I(X)\) denotes the *interpretation* of \(X\) in \(\mathrm{D}\) based on \(I\).

** Two important facts

1. If \(X\) has \(n\) *free variables*, then \(I(X)\) describes a set of \(n\)-tuples of elements of \(D\), i.e. an \(n\)-ary relation \(\mathcal{X}\) on \(D\).
   - \(X\) can be viewed as an \(n\)-place *predicate* in \(L\).
   - The *relation* \(\mathcal{X}\) is the interpretation of \(X\).
2. If \(X\) has *no free variables*, then \(I(X)\) is a statement about \(\mathrm{D}\) with a truth value: either *true* or *false*.

*** Summary

| Free variables in \(X\) | \(I(X)\)                                        |
|-------------------------+-------------------------------------------------|
| \(n \geq 1\)            | An \(n\)-ary relation \(\mathcal{X}\) on \(D\)  |
| none (a sentence)       | A statement about \(\mathrm{D}\): true or false |

** Using first-order languages in applications

- In practice we usually already know the structure \(\mathrm{D}\) we want to "talk" about using FOL.
- So we define a language \(L\) with enough predicates and constants to say what we need about \(\mathrm{D}\).


** Interpreting quantifiers

The *interpretation* of a formula \(X\) /can/ depend on the *order* in which its *quantifiers* are interpreted, so we need a *convention*.

*** Convention

A /sequence/ of *quantifiers* is always interpreted *from left-to-right* in a formula \(X\).

- If /all/ *quantifiers* in a sequence are of the *same type* (all \(\forall\), e.g. \((\forall x), (\forall y)\), or all \(\exists\), e.g. \((\exists x), (\exists y)\)), then the interpretation of \(X\) is the *same regardless of their order*.
- If the types are /mixed/, the *order can change the interpretation*.
