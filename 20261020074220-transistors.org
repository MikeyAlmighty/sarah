#+title: Transistors

* Transistors

** Overview

The single most important hardware component of a computer is the *transistor*.

Modern computers contain /billions/ of them.

- *Moore's law*: an empirical result saying the *number of transistors* in *integrated circuits* /doubles/ roughly every /two years/.
- Invented in the *1940s*, built from semiconducting materials such as ~silicon~.
- How they work is complex and relies on quantum-mechanical properties of ~semiconductors~, but what they /do/ is very simple.

** Function

The basic function of a *transistor* is to *amplify a signal*: a weak /input/ signal controls a large /output/ signal.

An extreme case is where the /input/ turns the large signal fully ~on~ or ~off~, i.e. the *transistor* is used as an *electronic switch*.

This is the most important use of *transistors* in computers.

** Mathematical model

A *transistor* is a device with one /input/, one *control* (/input/) and one /output/:

- If ~0~ is input to the *control*, the switch is *closed* and the output equals the /input/.
- If ~1~ is input to the *control*, the switch is *open* and the output is ~0~ irrespective of the /input/.

It can be regarded as a new *Boolean operation* written \(x \,\square\, y\), where \(x\) is the *input* and \(y\) the *control*.

| x | y | x □ y |
|---+---+-------|
| 1 | 1 |     0 |
| 1 | 0 |     1 |
| 0 | 1 |     0 |
| 0 | 0 |     0 |

** Theorem 5.3 (fundamental theorem of transistors)

A *transistor* on its own doesn't accomplish much, but very complicated behaviour can be achieved by connecting *transistors* together into *circuits*.

- Every *combinational circuit* can be constructed from *transistors*.
