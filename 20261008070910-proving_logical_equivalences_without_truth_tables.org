#+title: Proving Logical Equivalences Without Truth Tables


* Proving Logical Equivalences Without Truth Tables

** Idea

Transform one [[id:29ce4b76-3cfc-46ee-9ed9-74c2f91da633][WFF]] into the other by repeatedly replacing components with *logically equivalent* ones.

Every step needs a stated reason (a known equivalence), so the chain is a "legal" proof.

** Key equivalence (★)

\[ p \to q \equiv \lnot p \lor q \]
Used to eliminate \( \to \), so the standard laws (*De Morgan*, *distributivity*, ...) can be applied.

** Toolbox

- *Commutativity*, *associativity*, *distributivity* (Theorem 4.1)
- *De Morgan*: \( \lnot(a \lor b) \equiv \lnot a \land \lnot b \)
- *Double negation*: \( \lnot\lnot a \equiv a \)
- *Complement*: \( \lnot q \lor q \equiv \mathbf{t} \)
- *Identity*: \( a \land \mathbf{t} \equiv a \)

** Strategy

1. Start from the left-hand side.
2. Rewrite \( \to \) using (★).
3. Apply *De Morgan*, *double negation*, *distributivity*, etc.
4. Stop when you reach the right-hand side.
5. Write a reason beside every line.

** Example 1

Show \( (p \to q) \land (p \to r) \equiv p \to (q \land r) \).

\begin{align*}
(p \to q) \land (p \to r) &\equiv (\lnot p \lor q) \land (\lnot p \lor r) && \text{by } (\star) \\
&\equiv \lnot p \lor (q \land r) && \text{by distributivity} \\
&\equiv p \to (q \land r) && \text{by } (\star)
\end{align*}

** Example 2

Show \( (p \to q) \to q \equiv p \lor q \).

\begin{align*}
(p \to q) \to q &\equiv \lnot(p \to q) \lor q && \text{by } (\star) \\
&\equiv \lnot(\lnot p \lor q) \lor q && \text{by } (\star) \\
&\equiv (\lnot\lnot p \land \lnot q) \lor q && \text{by De Morgan} \\
&\equiv (p \land \lnot q) \lor q && \text{by double negation} \\
&\equiv (p \lor q) \land (\lnot q \lor q) && \text{by distributivity} \\
&\equiv (p \lor q) \land \mathbf{t} && \text{since } \lnot q \lor q \equiv \mathbf{t} \\
&\equiv p \lor q && \text{by identity}
\end{align*}

** Notes

- Eliminating \( \to \) first is the usual opening move.
- *Distributivity* works in both directions: it can factor /out/ (Example 1) or /expand/ (Example 2).
