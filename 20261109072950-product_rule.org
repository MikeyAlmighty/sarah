#+title: Product Rule

* Product Rule

** Statement

If there are \(n(A)\) ways to do \(A\) and \(n(B)\) ways to do \(B\), then the number of ways to do \(A\) *and* \(B\) is

\[ n(A) \times n(B) \]

Assumes \(A\) and \(B\) are *independent*: the number of choices for \(B\) is the same whatever choice was made for \(A\).

Extends to a sequence of choices:
- \(n(A) \times n(B) \times n(C) \times \dots\)

** Key idea

- Keyword: *AND* — a sequence of choices, one after another.
- Set form: \(|A \times B| = |A| \cdot |B|\).
- Think of a decision tree: each branch at step 1 splits into the same number of branches at step 2.

** Example: Student Committee

One student from each of 1st (500), 2nd (300) and 3rd (100) year:

\[ 500 \times 300 \times 100 = 15\,000\,000 \]

** Example: walking A -> B -> C -> D

5 routes A -> B, 3 routes B -> C, 2 routes C -> D.

Must do all three legs:

\[ 5 \times 3 \times 2 = 30 \]

** Example: File Names (with [[id:d92eb942-8015-40f8-846b-734525a9a77b][Sum Rule]])

Length-3 name: first symbol a letter (26), next two letter or digit (36 each):
\(26 \times 36 \times 36 = 33\,696\).
Combine lengths 1–3 with the sum rule: \(26 + 936 + 33\,696 = 34\,658\).

** Sum vs product

| Rule    | Keyword | Condition   | Operation  |
|---------+---------+-------------+------------|
| Sum     | OR      | disjoint    | \(+\)      |
| Product | AND     | independent | \(\times\) |
