#+title: Algebra in Boolean Algebra

* Algebra in Boolean Algebra

** Idea

*Boolean algebra* is similar to, but also different from, the kind of algebra you learnt at school.

Just how different is illustrated by the following results that will be ~true~ in /all/ *Boolean algebras* because they are proved from the *Boolean algebra axioms*.

** Proposition 5.1

Let $B$ be a Boolean algebra and let $a, b \in B$.

| # | Identity                                                      | Name                                   |
|---+---------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------|
| 1 | $a^2 = a \cdot a = a$                                         | *Idempotence*                          |
| 2 | $a + a = a$                                                   | *Idempotence*                          |
| 3 | $a \cdot 0 = 0$                                               | $0$ is the *zero* for *multiplication* |
| 4 | $1 + a = 1$                                                   | $1$ is the *zero* for *addition*       |
| 5 | $a = a + a \cdot b$                                           | *Absorption law*                       |
| 6 | $a + b = a + \overline{a} \cdot b = a + b \cdot \overline{a}$ | *Difference law*                       |

Not all proved in the text; only #1 is shown, a few more are left as exercises.

** Proof of idempotence of multiplication (#1)

\begin{align*}
a &= a \cdot 1                      && \text{(B6)} \\
  &= a \cdot (a + \overline{a})     && \text{(B9)} \\
  &= a \cdot a + a \cdot \overline{a} && \text{(B7)} \\
  &= a^2 + 0                        && \text{(B10)} \\
  &= a^2                            && \text{(B3)}
\end{align*}

** Proof of Absorption Law (#5)

*** Claim

*Absorption law* (Proposition 5.1, item 5): for all $a, b \in B$,

\[ a = a + a \cdot b \]

*** Proof

\begin{align*}
a &= a \cdot 1          && \text{(B6)} \\
  &= a \cdot (1 + b)    && \text{(Prop 5.1 #4: } 1 + b = 1 \text{)} \\
  &= a \cdot 1 + a \cdot b && \text{(B7)} \\
  &= a + a \cdot b      && \text{(B6)}
\end{align*}

*** Notes

- The "(4)" in the lecture proof is *Prop 5.1 item 4* ($1 + a = 1$), not axiom B4 (associativity of $\cdot$).
  - It is a derived result, not an axiom.
- Strategy: write $a$ as $a \cdot 1$, rewrite $1$ as $1 + b$, then distribute with B7.
