#+title: Closures of Relations

* Closures of Relations

** Idea

The *closure* of a relation $R$ with respect to a property $P$ is the /smallest/ relation that
1. contains $R$, and
2. has property $P$.

So we add only the pairs that are forced by $P$ and nothing else.

In CS, closures answer *reachability* questions: can we get from node $a$ to node $b$?

** Reflexive closure

- Definition 7.11 ::
  The *reflexive closure* $R_r$ of a relation $R$ on $A$ is the /smallest/ relation on $A$ that is reflexive and contains $R$.
- Construction ::
  Add every pair $(a,a)$ with $a \in A$:
  \[ R_r = R \cup \{(a,a) \mid a \in A\} \]

*** Example

$A = \{1,2,3,4\}$, $R = \{(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,3),(3,4)\}$

Missing loops: $(2,2), (3,3), (4,4)$.

\[ R_r = \{(1,1),(1,2),(2,2),(1,3),(2,3),(3,3),(3,4),(4,4)\} \]

** Symmetric closure

- Definition 7.12 :: The *symmetric closure* $R_s$ of a relation $R$ on $A$ is the smallest relation on $A$ that is symmetric and contains $R$.
- Construction :: For each $(a,b) \in R$ with $a \neq b$, add the reverse pair $(b,a)$ (if not already present):
  \[ R_s = R \cup \{(b,a) \mid (a,b) \in R,\ a \neq b\} \]
  (The $a \neq b$ condition is cosmetic: reversing $(a,a)$ gives $(a,a)$ again.)

*** Example
$A = \{1,2,3,4\}$, $R = \{(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,3),(3,4)\}$

Reverses to add: $(2,1), (3,1), (3,2), (4,3)$.

\[ R_s = \{(1,1),(1,2),(2,1),(1,3),(3,1),(2,3),(3,2),(3,4),(4,3)\} \]

** Transitive closure

- Definition 7.13 :: The *transitive closure* $R_t$ of a relation $R$ on $A$ is the smallest relation on $A$ that is transitive and contains $R$.
- Construction (iterative) ::
  1. For every pair of pairs $(a,b) \in R$ and $(b,c) \in R$, add $(a,c)$:
     \[ R_1 = R \cup \{(a,c) \mid (a,b) \in R \text{ and } (b,c) \in R\} \]
  2. Repeat on $R_1$ to get $R_2$, and so on.
  3. Stop when nothing new is added, i.e. $R_n = R_{n+1}$. Then $R_t := R_n$.

Unlike the other two, one pass is *not* always enough — new pairs can create new chains.

*** Example

$A = \{1,2,3\}$, $R = \{(1,1),(1,2),(2,3),(3,2)\}$

Pass 1 — look for chains $(a,b),(b,c)$:
| chain         | add     |
|---------------+---------|
| $(1,2),(2,3)$ | $(1,3)$ |
| $(2,3),(3,2)$ | $(2,2)$ |
| $(3,2),(2,3)$ | $(3,3)$ |

\[ R_1 = \{(1,1),(1,2),(2,3),(1,3),(3,2),(2,2),(3,3)\} \]

Pass 2 - new chains such as $(1,3),(3,2)$ give $(1,2)$, already present.

Nothing new, so $R_2 = R_1$.

\[ R_t = \{(1,1),(1,2),(2,3),(1,3),(3,2),(2,2),(3,3)\} \]

** Summary

| Closure    | Property   | What to add                          |             Passes |
|------------+------------+--------------------------------------+--------------------|
| Reflexive  | reflexive  | $(a,a)$ for all $a \in A$            |                  1 |
| Symmetric  | symmetric  | $(b,a)$ for each $(a,b) \in R$       |                  1 |
| Transitive | transitive | $(a,c)$ for each chain $(a,b),(b,c)$ | repeat till stable |

** Pitfalls

- Reflexive closure depends on $A$, not just $R$: elements of $A$ that never appear in $R$ still need their loop $(a,a)$.
- Transitive closure: don't stop after one pass - check that a further pass adds nothing.
- Chains through loops (e.g. $(1,1),(1,2)$) only give back pairs already in $R$; safe to skip.
