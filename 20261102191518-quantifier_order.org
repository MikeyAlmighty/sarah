#+title: Quantifier Order

* Quantifier Order

** Rules
- \((\forall x)\): the statement must hold for *every* element \(x\) of the domain \(D\).
- \((\exists x)\): the statement must hold for *at least one* element \(x\) of \(D\).
- *Quantifiers* are read *left to right*.
  - A later quantifier *may depend on* earlier variables.
  - An earlier one *cannot depend on* later variables.
- Same type (\(\forall\forall\) or \(\exists\exists\)): order doesn't matter.
- Mixed (\(\forall\) and \(\exists\)): order can change the truth value.

** The key pair

| Form           | Says                                   | Dependence                   |
|----------------+----------------------------------------+------------------------------|
| \((\forall x)(\exists y)\) | for each \(x\) there is some \(y\)     | \(y\) may depend on \(x\)    |
| \((\exists y)(\forall x)\) | one single \(y\) works for every \(x\) | \(y\) cannot depend on \(x\) |

The second is a much stronger claim.

If \((\exists y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\) is ~true~, then \((\forall x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\) is ~true~, but not the other way round.

** Worked example: \(\mathbb{N}\) with \(P(x,y)\): \(x \leq y\)

*** \((\forall x)(\exists y)(x \leq y)\): True
For each \(m\) there is an \(n\) with \(m \leq n\). Take \(n = m + 1\). There is no largest natural number.

*** \((\exists y)(\forall x)(x \leq y)\): False
There is a \(y\) that is at least as big as every \(x\), i.e. a largest natural number.

Disproof: take any \(y\).

Then \(x = y + 1\) is in \(\mathbb{N}\) and \(x \leq y\) fails.

This works for every \(y\), so no \(y\) works.

** Worked example: people with \(P(x,y)\): "\(x\) is the mother of \(y\)"

*** \((\forall y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\): True
Everyone has a mother. Each \(y\) gets its own \(x\) (if \(D\) isn't restricted to living people).

*** \((\exists y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\): False
There is one person whose mother is everyone. Impossible.

** How to check a sentence

1. Say it in words, left to right, using *"for every"* and *"there exists"*.
2. For \(\forall\): try to find *one counterexample*. If you find one, the sentence is false.
3. For \(\exists\): try to find *one witness*. If you find one, the sentence is true.
4. If a witness comes *after* a \(\forall\), it is allowed to depend on that variable (e.g. \(y = x + 1\)).
5. If a witness comes *before* a \(\forall\), it must work for all values (no dependence).

** Common mistakes

- Treating \((\forall x)(\exists y)\) and \((\exists y)(\forall x)\) as the same. They are not.
- Forgetting that the *interpretation* decides truth: same sentence, different domain or relation, possibly different truth value.
- Thinking constants are variables. Constants (like \(a\)) are never free or bound.
