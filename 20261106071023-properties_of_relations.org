#+title: Properties of Relations

* Properties of Relations

** Definition 7.9

Let $R$ be a *relation* on a set $A$.

$R$ is
- *Reflexive* if $(a,a) \in R$ for all $a \in A$;
- *Symmetric* if whenever $(a,b) \in R$ then $(b,a) \in R$;
- *Antisymmetric* if whenever $(a,b) \in R$ and $(b,a) \in R$ then $a = b$;
- *Transitive* if whenever $(a,b) \in R$ and $(b,c) \in R$ then $(a,c) \in R$.

*** Note

- A relation can be *both* *symmetric* and *antisymmetric* (e.g. the equality relation $\{(a,a) \mid a \in A\}$), or *neither*.
- *Antisymmetric* $\neq$ "not symmetric".
  - *Antisymmetric* means: no two /distinct/ elements are *related* in /both directions/.

** Examples per property

Let $A = \{1,2,3\}$.

| Property      | Example                       | Counterexample    | Reason for Counter            |
|---------------+-------------------------------+-------------------+-------------------------------|
| Reflexive     | $\{(1,1),(2,2),(3,3),(1,2)\}$ | $\{(1,1),(2,2)\}$ | $(3,3)$ missing               |
| Symmetric     | $\{(1,2),(2,1),(3,3)\}$       | $\{(1,2)\}$       | $(2,1)$ missing               |
| Antisymmetric | $\{(1,1),(1,2),(2,3)\}$       | $\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$ | $1 \neq 2$, related both ways |
| Transitive    | $\{(1,2),(2,3),(1,3)\}$       | $\{(1,2),(2,3)\}$ | $(1,3)$ missing               |

*** Edge cases

- *Both* symmetric and antisymmetric: $\{(1,1),(2,2)\}$
  - Only pairs of the form $(a,a)$, so both conditions hold.
- *Neither*: $\{(1,2),(2,1),(2,3)\}$
  - Not symmetric: $(3,2)$ missing.
  - Not antisymmetric: $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ with $1 \neq 2$.
- *Transitivity trap*: $\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$ is *not* transitive.
  - $(1,2),(2,1)$ requires $(1,1)$; $(2,1),(1,2)$ requires $(2,2)$.
- *Empty relation* $\varnothing$: symmetric, antisymmetric and transitive (vacuously, since there's no pair to break the rule), but *not* reflexive.

** Example 1: divisibility on $\{1,2,3,4\}$

$A = \{1,2,3,4\}$, $R = \{(a,b) \mid a \text{ divides } b\}$.

\[
R = \{(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,2),(3,3),(4,4),(2,4)\}
\]

| Property      | Holds? | Reason                                                     |
|---------------+--------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| Reflexive     | Yes    | Contains $(1,1),(2,2),(3,3),(4,4)$                         |
| Symmetric     | No     | $(1,2) \in R$ but $(2,1) \notin R$                         |
| Antisymmetric | Yes    | No $a \neq b$ with both $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$ in $R$         |
| Transitive    | Yes    | Only non-trivial chain is $(1,2),(2,4)$, and $(1,4) \in R$ |
|               |        |                                                            |

** Example 2: "likes" on people

$R = \{(a,b) \mid a \text{ likes } b\}$.

- Not *symmetric*:
  - $a$ liking $b$ doesn't mean $b$ likes $a$.
- Not *antisymmetric*:
  - two different people can like each other.
- Not *transitive*:
  - $a$ likes $b$ and $b$ likes $c$ doesn't mean $a$ likes $c$.
- Probably not *reflexive*: not everyone likes themselves.
