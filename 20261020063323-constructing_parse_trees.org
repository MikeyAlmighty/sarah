#+title: Constructing Parse Trees

* Constructing Parse Trees

** Idea

A *parse tree* is built step by step, mirroring how a *wff* is built by the *rules* for *wff*.

Each rule for *wff* has a /matching construction/ on *trees*.

A *parse tree* represents a *wff* without brackets.

** Construction rules

*** Fact 1: atoms (analogue of [[id:29ce4b76-3cfc-46ee-9ed9-74c2f91da633][WFF Rule 1]])

The parse tree of an atom $p$ is a single vertex labelled $p$.

#+begin_example
(p)
#+end_example

*** Fact 2: binary connectives (analogue of [[id:29ce4b76-3cfc-46ee-9ed9-74c2f91da633][WFF Rule 2]])

Let $X$, $Y$ be *wff* with *parse trees* $T_X$, $T_Y$, and let $*$ be any of the /five/ *binary connectives*.

Join the *roots* of $T_X$ and $T_Y$ to a new *vertex* labelled $*$.

The new *vertex* is the *root* of the *tree* for $X * Y$.

#+begin_example
     (*)
    /   \
 (T_X) (T_Y)
#+end_example

Example: tree for $p \vee q$

#+begin_example
   (∨)
   /  \
 (p)  (q)
#+end_example

*** Fact 3: negation

Attach the *root* of $T_X$ to a new *vertex* labelled $\neg$.

The new *vertex* is the *root* of the *tree* for $\neg X$.

#+begin_example
 (¬)
  |
 (T_X)
#+end_example

** Example 1: build the tree for $\neg(p \vee q) \vee r$

Follow the order in which the wff is built.

1. Form $p \vee q$ (Fact 2).
2. Form $\neg(p \vee q)$ by putting a new $\neg$ *vertex* above the $\vee$ *root* (Fact 3).
3. Form $\neg(p \vee q) \vee r$ by joining that *tree* and the *tree* for $r$ under a new $\vee$ *root* (Fact 2).

#+begin_example
       (∨)
       /  \
    (¬)   (r)
     |
    (∨)
    /  \
  (p)  (q)
#+end_example

** Example 2: read a wff off a tree

Read the *tree* *from the bottom up*, forming the *wff* as you move up.

#+begin_example
 (¬)
  |
 (¬)
  |
 (↔)
 /   \
(→)  (p)
/ \
(p)(p)
#+end_example

Bottom up:
1. $p \to p$ (the $\to$ *vertex*)
2. $(p \to p) \leftrightarrow p$ (the $\leftrightarrow$ *vertex*)
3. $\neg((p \to p) \leftrightarrow p)$, then $\neg\neg((p \to p) \leftrightarrow p)$

** Notes

- Building the *tree* and reading it off are inverse processes: build by /following/ the [[id:29ce4b76-3cfc-46ee-9ed9-74c2f91da633][WFF Rules]], read by *reversing* them.
- *Binary connectives* give /two/ children, $\neg$ gives /one/, *atoms* are *leaves*.
- *Order of construction* fixes the structure, which is why /no brackets/ are needed.
