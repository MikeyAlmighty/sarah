Irrational Numbers

Irrational Numbers

Some numbers cannot be expressed as fractions of integers.

These are called irrational numbers.

Two famous examples are:

  • \( \sqrt{2} \)
  • \( \pi \)

Geometrically:

  • \( \sqrt{2} \) is the length of the diagonal of a square with side length \(1\)
  • \( \pi \) is the circumference of a circle with diameter \(1\)

Another important irrational number is:

  • \( e \)

which is the base of the natural logarithm.

Unlike \( \sqrt{2} \) and \( \pi \), the number \( e \) does not have a simple geometric interpretation.

These numbers are examples of real numbers that are not rational.

Properties of irrational Numbers

In the case of irrational numbers, the decimal expansion:

  • does not terminate
  • does not eventually repeat

For example, the decimal representation of \( \pi \) begins as:

\[ 3.14159\ldots \]

but:

  • no finite sequence of digits represents \( \pi \) exactly
  • and its digits do not fall into a repeating pattern

Conversely, a decimal expansion that:

  • terminates, or
  • eventually repeats

must represent a rational number.

For example:

  • \(0.25 = \frac{1}{4}\) (terminating decimal)
  • \(0.333\ldots = \frac{1}{3}\) (repeating decimal)

Real Numbers as Decimals

For the scope of this course, the set \( \mathbb{R} \) of real numbers can be viewed as consisting of:

  • all terminating decimals
  • all repeating decimals
  • all non-repeating, non-terminating decimals

This provides a way to understand:

\[ \mathbb{R} \]

as the full number line, including both:

  • rational numbers
  • irrational numbers

Proof that \( \sqrt{2} \) is Irrational

We will prove that \( \sqrt{2} \) is irrational.

This is a classical example of Proof by Contradiction.

The Proof

Assume, for contradiction, that \( \sqrt{2} \) is rational.

Then there exist integers \(p\) and \(q > 0\) such that:

\[ \sqrt{2} = \frac{p}{q} \]

We may assume that \( \frac{p}{q} \) is in lowest terms.

That means:

  • \(p\) and \(q\) have no common divisors

Square both sides:

\[ 2 = \frac{p^2}{q^2} \]

Multiply both sides by \(q^2\):

\[ p^2 = 2q^2 \]

This shows that \(p^2\) is even.

But:

  • the square of an odd number is odd

Therefore:

  • \(p\) must be even

So there exists an integer \(k\) such that:

\[ p = 2k \]

Substitute into the equation:

\[ p^2 = 2q^2 \]

giving:

\[ (2k)^2 = 2q^2 \]

Hence:

\[ 4k^2 = 2q^2 \]

Divide both sides by \(2\):

\[ 2k^2 = q^2 \]

So \(q^2\) is also even.

Therefore:

  • \(q\) must also be even

The Contradiction

We have shown that:

  • \(p\) is even
  • \(q\) is even

So both are divisible by \(2\).

But this contradicts our earlier assumption that \( \frac{p}{q} \) was in lowest terms.

Therefore the assumption is false.

Hence \( \sqrt{2} \) is irrational.

Consequences

We have shown that:

  • \( \sqrt{2} \) cannot be expressed as a ratio of integers

More generally:

  • the square root of any natural number that is not a perfect square is irrational

Irrational numbers are still real numbers and can be represented using:

  • decimal expansions
  • positional notation

However:

  • their decimal expansions never terminate
  • and never repeat periodically
