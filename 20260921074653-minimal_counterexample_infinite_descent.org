#+title: Minimal Counterexample / Infinite Descent

* Minimal Counterexample / Infinite Descent

** Idea

A proof technique for showing *no* object with some "bad" property exists — closely related to [[id:8b1e8e5b-d981-41e0-93a7-eded048cd150][Mathematical Induction]], but framed as a [[id:c9b24884-e730-45da-bb3a-1ff7396e4645][Mathematical Contradiction]].
** The pattern

1. *Assume* a counterexample exists (something with the bad property).
2. Among all counterexamples, take the *smallest* one (by some well-defined measure —
   usually a positive integer, since positive integers can't decrease forever).
3. Show that this smallest counterexample can be used to construct an *even smaller*
   counterexample.
4. Contradiction — you already said it was the smallest. So no counterexample exists.

** Why it works

Relies on the fact that there's no infinite strictly-decreasing sequence of positive
integers. If a "smaller bad example" always exists, you could keep descending forever —
impossible. Hence step 3 breaking that is enough to kill the assumption in step 1.

** Relation to induction

Minimal counterexample and (strong) induction are two sides of the same coin — both rest
on the well-ordering of the positive integers. Induction builds *up* from a base case;
minimal counterexample / descent works *down* from an assumed counterexample. Often
either can be used for the same theorem — pick whichever reads more naturally.
