Interpreting FOL Formulae

Setup

Let \(L = (P, Q, a)\) be a simple first-order language with:

  • One unary predicate \(P\)
  • One binary predicate \(Q\)
  • One constant \(a\)

Example: \((\exists x)(P(x) \land Q(x,a))\) is a sentence in \(L\) (its only variable \(x\) is bound).

It only has a meaning once \(L\) is associated with a structure \(\mathrm{D}\):

  • Every predicate in \(L\) is associated with a relation in \(\mathrm{D}\)
  • Every constant in \(L\) is associated with a distinguished element of \(\mathrm{D}\)

Then every formula in \(L\) is interpreted as either a statement about \(\mathrm{D}\) or a relation on \(\mathrm{D}\).

Example: People

Structure \(\mathrm{D} = (D, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{Q}, \text{Alice})\):

  • \(D\): a fixed set of people
  • \(\mathcal{P}\) (unary): \(x \in \mathcal{P}\) if "\(x\) is a man"
  • \(\mathcal{Q}\) (binary): \((x,y) \in \mathcal{Q}\) if "\(x\) is a child of \(y\)"
  • Alice: a distinguished element of \(D\)

Interpretation \(I\)

The correspondence between \(L\) and \(\mathrm{D}\):

  • \(P \mapsto \mathcal{P}\)
  • \(Q \mapsto \mathcal{Q}\)
  • \(a \mapsto \text{Alice}\)

This correspondence \(I\) is an interpretation of the language \(L\).

We use it to interpret formulae in \(L\) in terms of \(\mathrm{D}\).

Interpreting a sentence

\((\exists x)(P(x) \land Q(x,a))\) becomes

\[ \exists x \in D \text{ such that } (x \in \mathcal{P}) \land ((x,\text{Alice}) \in \mathcal{Q}) \]

i.e. "there is a man in \(D\) who is Alice's son", or "Alice has a son".

Interpreting an open formula

\((P(x) \land Q(x,a))\) becomes

\[ (x \in \mathcal{P}) \land ((x,\text{Alice}) \in \mathcal{Q}) \]

i.e. "\(x\) is the son of Alice".

Note: this describes a unary relation \(\mathcal{S}\) on \(D\), defined by

\[ x \in \mathcal{S} \iff (x \in \mathcal{P}) \land ((x,\text{Alice}) \in \mathcal{Q}) \]

Remark 1: Interpretations are not unique

The same \(L = (P, Q, a)\) can be interpreted in a different structure \(\mathrm{D}' = (D', \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{Q}, \text{Sun})\):

  • \(D'\): the set of all stars in the universe
  • \(\mathcal{P}\) (unary): \(\mathcal{P}(x)\) means "\(x\) is a white dwarf"
  • \(\mathcal{Q}\) (binary): \(\mathcal{Q}(x,y)\) means "\(x\) and \(y\) are part of the same galaxy"
  • Sun: a distinguished element of \(D'\)

Interpretation \(I'\):

  • \(P \mapsto \mathcal{P}\)
  • \(Q \mapsto \mathcal{Q}\)
  • \(a \mapsto \text{Sun}\)

Key point: when using a first-order language to "talk" about a structure, we must be clear about:

  • The correspondence between predicates in the language and relations in the structure
  • The correspondence between constants in the language and distinguished elements in the structure

Remark 2: Sentences vs Open Formulae

Formula type Example Interpretation
Sentence \((\exists x)(P(x) \land Q(x,a))\) A statement about \(\mathrm{D}\): true or false
Open formula \((P(x) \land Q(x,a))\) A relation on the domain \(D\)
