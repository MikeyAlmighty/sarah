#+title: Functional Completeness

* Functional Completeness

* Motivation
- [[id:13b59854-77dc-4572-a114-0db15f2f7a3e][Truth Functions in PL]] were introduced in order to state a theorem about the range of expressions possible in the *language of PL*.

* Theorem 8.2 (Functional completeness)

For each truth function \(f : \mathbb{T}^n \to \mathbb{T}\) there is a wff with \(n\) distinct atoms whose truth table corresponds to the truth table of \(f\).

- Additional connectives would *not* let us construct more truth functions as wffs.
- They would not make PL more expressive - they would only provide more logically equivalent expressions.

* Example

Suppose \(f : \mathbb{T}^3 \to \mathbb{T}\) has the truth table:

| Row |   |   |   | \(f(t)\) |
|-----+---+---+---+----------|
|   1 | T | T | T | T        |
|   2 | T | T | F | F        |
|   3 | T | F | T | F        |
|   4 | T | F | F | T        |
|   5 | F | T | T | T        |
|   6 | F | T | F | F        |
|   7 | F | F | T | F        |
|   8 | F | F | F | F        |

Goal: find a wff \(A\) with three atoms \(p\), \(q\), \(r\) with the same truth table:

| Row | \(p\) | \(q\) | \(r\) | \(A\) |
|-----+-------+-------+-------+-------|
|   1 | T     | T     | T     | T     |
|   2 | T     | T     | F     | F     |
|   3 | T     | F     | T     | F     |
|   4 | T     | F     | F     | T     |
|   5 | F     | T     | T     | T     |
|   6 | F     | T     | F     | F     |
|   7 | F     | F     | T     | F     |
|   8 | F     | F     | F     | F     |

* Strategy for constructing \(A\)

1. Consider only the rows where the truth value is \(T\) (rows 1, 4, 5).
2. For each such row, form its *[[id:4d46fa7e-f04d-4e1e-82fa-f2cdb9de34d1][Conjunctive Clause]]*: *conjoin* each atom, *negated* if its value in that row is \(F\).
   - *Row 1* \((T,T,T)\): \(p \land q \land r\) — true exactly when \(p, q, r\) are \(T, T, T\).
   - *Row 4* \((T,F,F)\): \(p \land \lnot q \land \lnot r\) — true exactly when \(p, q, r\) are \(T, F, F\).
   - *Row 5* \((F,T,T)\): \(\lnot p \land q \land r\) — true exactly when \(p, q, r\) are \(F, T, T\).
3. Form the *disjunction* of these *[[id:4d46fa7e-f04d-4e1e-82fa-f2cdb9de34d1][Conjunctive Clause]]*:
   \[ A = (p \land q \land r) \lor (p \land \lnot q \land \lnot r) \lor (\lnot p \land q \land r) \]
   - \(A\) is the *full (canonical) Disjunctive Normal Form (DNF)* of \(f\):
     - a *disjunction of conjunctive clauses*, each containing every atom exactly once, with one clause per \(T\) row.
     - A *DNF* is not unique, e.g. merging rows 1 and 5 gives the shorter, equivalent DNF \((q \land r) \lor (p \land \lnot q \land \lnot r)\).

The truth table of \(A\) matches that of \(f\).

This construction also gives a good idea of how *Theorem 8.2* could be proved.
