#+title: The Cartesian Product

* The Cartesian Product

- Used later to define relations, functions, and coordinate systems.

** Definition 7.3: Ordered pair

An *ordered pair* $(a, b)$ is a pair where $a$ comes before $b$.

*** Equality:

$(a, b) = (c, d) \iff a = c \land b = d$
- $(3, 8) \neq (8, 3)$

*** Pair vs set

| Notation   | Order matters? | Example               |
|------------+----------------+-----------------------|
| $(a, b)$   | yes            | $(3, 8) \neq (8, 3)$  |
| $\{a, b\}$ | no             | $\{3, 8\} = \{8, 3\}$ |

** Definition 7.4: Cartesian product

$A \times B = \{(a, b) \mid a \in A \land b \in B\}$

** Example

$A = \{1, 2, 3\}$, $B = \{x, y\}$
$A \times B = \{(1,x), (1,y), (2,x), (2,y), (3,x), (3,y)\}$

*** Method:

- Fix each $a \in A$ in turn, pair it with every $b \in B$.

** Properties

Not in the course text yet, but follow from the definition:
- Size :: $|A \times B| = |A| \cdot |B|$ (example: $3 \cdot 2 = 6$)
- Not commutative :: $A \times B \neq B \times A$ in general ($(1,x) \in A \times B$, but $(1,x) \notin B \times A$)
- With $\varnothing$ :: $A \times \varnothing = \varnothing$ (no $b$ to pair with)
