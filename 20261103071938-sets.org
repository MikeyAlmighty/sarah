#+title: Sets

* Sets

** Why sets

*Set theory* is the foundation of the language of *mathematics*, and so of *CS*.

** Definition 7.1

A *set* is an unordered collection of objects.

** Membership

- $a \in A$ :: $a$ *is* an element of $A$
- $a \notin A$ :: $a$ *is not* an element of $A$

*** Example:

$A = \{1, 3, 5, 7\}$
- gives $5 \in A$
- and $6 \notin A$

** Notation

*** Roster (listing)

$A = \{2, 4, 6, 8, \dots\}$

*** Set-builder

$A = \{x \mid x \text{ is a positive even number}\}$
- $\mid$ is read "such that"

** Key properties

*** No duplicates

Elements are counted *once*: $\{a, b, b, c, c, c\} = \{a, b, c\}$

*** No order

$\{a, b, c\} = \{c, a, b\}$

** Equality

$A = B$ iff $A$ and $B$ contain exactly the *same elements*.

*** Formally:

$A = B \iff \forall x\,(x \in A \leftrightarrow x \in B)$

** CS link

Like a *hash set*: membership is all that matters - no duplicates, no ordering.
