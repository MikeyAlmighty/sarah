Interpreting Sentences (Examples)

Setup

Sentences involving the 2-place predicate symbol \(P\):

1. \((\forall x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\) 2. \((\forall x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\)
3. \((\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\) 4. \((\exists x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\)
5. \((\forall y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\) 6. \((\exists y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\)
7. \((\forall y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\) 8. \((\exists y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\)

Interpretation:

  • Domain \(D\): the set of all people
  • \(P(x,y)\): "\(x\) is the mother of \(y\)"

Shortcuts

Quantifier order doesn't matter when all quantifiers are the same type (see Understanding Interpretations in FOL):

  • Sentence 5 \(\equiv\) sentence 1, so only interpret 1.
  • Sentence 8 \(\equiv\) sentence 4, so only interpret 4.

Interpretations

  1. \((\forall x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\): for any two people \(a\) and \(b\), \(a\) is the mother of \(b\). False.
  2. \((\forall x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\): every \(a\) is the mother of at least one person, i.e. everyone is a mother. False.
  3. \((\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\): there is someone \(a\) who is the mother of every person. False.
  4. \((\exists x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\): there is a person \(a\) and a person \(b\) such that \(a\) is the mother of \(b\). True.
  5. \((\forall y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\): same as sentence 1 (order doesn't matter). False.
  6. \((\exists y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\): there is someone for whom everyone is a mother. False.
  7. \((\forall y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\): everyone has a mother. True (if \(D\) is not restricted to living people).
  8. \((\exists y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\): same as sentence 4 (order doesn't matter). True.

Summary

# Sentence Reads as Truth
1 \((\forall x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\) everyone is everyone's mother F
2 \((\forall x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\) everyone is a mother F
3 \((\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\) someone is everyone's mother F
4 \((\exists x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\) someone is a mother T
5 \((\forall y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\) same as 1 F
6 \((\exists y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\) someone has everyone as mother F
7 \((\forall y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\) everyone has a mother T
8 \((\exists y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\) same as 4 T

Takeaway:

  • With mixed quantifiers, order matters.
  • Sentences 6 and 7 use the same quantifiers on the same variables, only swapped (\((\exists y)(\forall x)\) vs \((\forall y)(\exists x)\)), yet 6 is false and 7 is true.

Second interpretation: Natural Numbers

Same eight sentences, different interpretation:

  • Domain \(D'\): \(\mathbb{N}\), the set of natural numbers
  • \(P(x,y)\): "\(x \leq y\)"

Interpretations

  1. \((\forall x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\) becomes \((\forall x)(\forall y)(x \leq y)\): for any two natural numbers \(m\) and \(n\), we always have \(m \leq n\). False, e.g. not true for \(m = 2\) and \(n = 1\).
  2. \((\forall x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\) becomes \((\forall x)(\exists y)(x \leq y)\): for each natural number \(m\) there is a natural number \(n\) with \(m \leq n\), i.e. there is no largest natural number. True.
  3. \((\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\) becomes \((\exists x)(\forall y)(x \leq y)\): there is a smallest natural number. True, since \(0\) is the smallest (or \(1\), depending on the definition of \(\mathbb{N}\)).
  4. \((\exists x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\) becomes \((\exists x)(\exists y)(x \leq y)\): there are natural numbers \(m\) and \(n\) with \(m \leq n\). True, e.g. \(1 \leq 1\).
  5. \((\forall y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\): same as sentence 1 (order doesn't matter). False.
  6. \((\exists y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\) becomes \((\exists y)(\forall x)(x \leq y)\): there is a largest natural number. False.
  7. \((\forall y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\) becomes \((\forall y)(\exists x)(x \leq y)\): given any natural number \(n\) you can find a natural number \(m\) with \(m \leq n\). True, since \(n \leq n\).
  8. \((\exists y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\): same as sentence 4 (order doesn't matter). True.

Comparison

1 \((\forall x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\) F F
2 \((\forall x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\) F T
3 \((\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\) F T
4 \((\exists x)(\exists y)P(x,y)\) T T
5 \((\forall y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\) F F
6 \((\exists y)(\forall x)P(x,y)\) F F
7 \((\forall y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\) T T
8 \((\exists y)(\exists x)P(x,y)\) T T

Remarks

Depending on the interpretation of our language \(L\), a sentence \(X\) which is true in one interpretation may be false in a different interpretation.

For example, sentence 2 is false in the first interpretation (people, "mother of") but true in the second (\(\mathbb{N}\), \(\leq\)).
