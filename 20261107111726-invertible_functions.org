#+title: Invertible Functions

* Invertible Functions

** Definition (Def 8.5)

Let $f : A \to B$ and $g : B \to A$. If

- $g \circ f : A \to A$ is the [[id:a8322b84-1bde-4853-b716-5c2ce6260bf6][Identity Function]] on $A$, and
- $f \circ g : B \to B$ is the [[id:a8322b84-1bde-4853-b716-5c2ce6260bf6][Identity Function]] on $B$,

Then $f$ is the *inverse* of $g$ (and $g$ is the inverse of $f$).

A function that has an inverse is *invertible*. The inverse is written $f^{-1}$.

*Both compositions* must give the *identity* — checking one direction is not enough.

** Characterisation (Theorem 8.1)

\[ f \text{ is invertible} \iff f \text{ is injective and surjective (bijective)} \]

- *Injective (one-to-one):* each output comes from exactly one input.
- *Surjective (onto):* every element of the codomain is hit.

Invertible functions capture *reversibility* and are key to solving equations.

** Diagram

#+begin_example
          A                        B
       .-----.        f         .-----.
      /       \  ----------->  /       \
     |    x •  |               |  • f(x) |
      \       /  <-----------  \       /
       '-----'     g = f⁻¹      '-----'

     g ∘ f = i_A                f ∘ g = i_B
#+end_example

** Example

Let $f, g : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $f(x) = 3x + 1$ and $g(x) = \frac{x-1}{3}$.
Show $f \circ g = i$ and $g \circ f = i$.

\begin{align*}
(f \circ g)(x) &= f(g(x)) \\
               &= f\!\left(\tfrac{x-1}{3}\right) \\
               &= 3\left(\tfrac{x-1}{3}\right) + 1 \\
               &= (x-1) + 1 \\
               &= x = i(x)
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
(g \circ f)(x) &= g(f(x)) \\
               &= g(3x+1) \\
               &= \frac{(3x+1) - 1}{3} \\
               &= \frac{3x}{3} \\
               &= x = i(x)
\end{align*}

where $i$ is the identity function on $\mathbb{R}$.

By Def 8.5, $f$ is *invertible* with inverse $g$ (and $g$ is *invertible* with inverse $f$).
