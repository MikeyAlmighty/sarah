#+title: Logical Constants t and f

* Logical Constants t and f

** Logical constants

The logical constants are two special wff:
- \(t\): always *true* (a *tautology*)
- \(f\): always *false* (a *contradiction*)

They contain no atoms, and their truth tables are constant:

| t |
|---|
| T |

| f |
|---|
| F |

** Using them with equivalence

Via [[id:6154da29-5742-4917-8eb7-63d279178435][Definition of Logical Equivalence]], a wff \(A\) can be compared with a constant:

- \(A \equiv t\): \(A\) is a *tautology*. Its truth table is ~true~ under every assignment.
- \(A \equiv f\): \(A\) is a *contradiction*. Its truth table is ~false~ under every assignment.

A wff that is neither is called a *[[id:82f83ff4-d3ae-4020-88db-d15a1b1959fc][Contingency]]*: true under some assignments and false under others.

** Examples

*** Tautology: \(p \lor \neg p \equiv t\)

| p | ¬p | p ∨ ¬p |
|---+----+--------|
| T | F  | T      |
| F | T  | T      |

*** Contradiction: \(p \land \neg p \equiv f\)

| p | ¬p | p ∧ ¬p |
|---+----+--------|
| T | F  | F      |
| F | T  | F      |

** Notes

- Every tautology is logically equivalent to every other tautology (same truth table: all T). Likewise for contradictions.
- \(A\) is a tautology exactly when \(\neg A\) is a contradiction.
