#+title: Logical Constants and Tautologies

* Logical Constants and Tautologies

** Idea

Define the logical constants \(\mathbf{f}\) and \(\mathbf{t}\) and use them to give another way of characterising *tautologies* and *contradictions*.

** The logical constants

- Let \(\mathbf{f}\) be any wff that is always ~false~ (*contradiction*)
- Let \(\mathbf{t}\) be any wff that is always ~true~ (*tautology*).

*** Remarks

- Logical constants are used in wff in much the same way as atoms.
- If \(A\) is a *contradiction* then \(A \equiv \mathbf{f}\); if \(A\) is a *tautology* then \(A \equiv \mathbf{t}\).

** Example

Show that:
1. \(p \lor \neg p \equiv \mathbf{t}\)
2. \(p \land \neg p \equiv \mathbf{f}\)
3. \(p \to \mathbf{f} \equiv \neg p\)

*** Example 1: \(p \lor \neg p \equiv \mathbf{t}\)

Need: \(p \lor \neg p\) is always true. Clear from the truth table:

| p | ¬p | p ∨ ¬p |
|---+----+--------|
| T | F  | T      |
| F | T  | T      |

So \(p \lor \neg p \equiv \mathbf{t}\).

*** Example 2: \(p \land \neg p \equiv \mathbf{f}\)

Need: \(p \land \neg p\) is always false. Clear from the truth table:

| p | ¬p | p ∧ ¬p |
|---+----+--------|
| T | F  | F      |
| F | T  | F      |

So \(p \land \neg p \equiv \mathbf{f}\).

*** Example 3: \(p \to \mathbf{f} \equiv \neg p\)

It is enough to show that \((p \to \mathbf{f}) \leftrightarrow \neg p\) is a tautology:

| p | f | p → f | ¬p | (p → f) ↔ ¬p |
|---+---+-------+----+--------------|
| T | F | F     | F  | T            |
| F | F | T     | T  | T            |

Since \((p \to \mathbf{f}) \leftrightarrow \neg p\) is a tautology, \(p \to \mathbf{f} \equiv \neg p\).
